# New York — Appellate Litigation (Appellate Division, First and Second Departments)

Appellate Division forms for the First and Second Departments, plus the NYSCEF appellate-module form set.

> ## ⚠️ WARNING — AD1 AND AD2 FORMS DIFFER AND MUST NEVER BE CROSS-USED
>
> **The departments differ.** Practice Rules of the Appellate Division are statewide (22 NYCRR Part 1250), but each Department layers on its own local rules and, critically, **its own forms with different names, content and filing mechanics**.
>
> - The **First Department** publishes a *Note of Issue* and a *Summary Statement on Application for Expedited Service/Interim Relief*, and does **not** publish a Notice of Appeal form (the notice of appeal is filed in the court of original instance).
> - The **Second Department** publishes a *Notice of Appeal*, *Notice of Motion*, *Order to Show Cause*, *Informational Statement* (its perfection/RJI-analogue), *CPLR 5531 statement*, brief/record forms and its own e-filing form set.
> - The First Dept. Note of Issue has **no Second Dept. counterpart**; the First Dept. Summary Statement has **no Second Dept. analogue**; the Second Dept. Notice of Appeal has **no First Dept. equivalent** — this asymmetry is the single most common cross-department error.
> - The *Informational Statement* name exists in **both** Departments but is a different document with a different filing trigger — do not swap them.
> - The Second Dept. publishes appellant's-brief and record-on-appeal cover forms; the First Dept. publishes no equivalent cover-form set (formatting there is governed by 22 NYCRR 1250.8 and the First Dept. technical requirements).
>
> **Never reuse a Second Department form in the First Department or vice versa.**
>
> Both Departments' current forms pages were confirmed live in the research session: [First Department Court Rules and Forms](https://www.nycourts.gov/courts/ad1/Practice&Procedures/index.shtml) (screenshot) and [Second Department Forms & Practice Aids](https://www.nycourts.gov/courts/ad2/formsandpracticeaids.shtml) (screenshot). nycourts.gov blocks automated fetching, so document URLs below are live `href` values read from archived snapshots of the official pages.
